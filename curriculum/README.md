# Curriculum

Subject content and the plan behind it. Everything a student reads here is
Ukrainian; the planning documents a parent reads are English.

| Path | Contents | Language |
|---|---|---|
| `why/` | Honest answers to «навіщо мені це вчити», one file per subject | Ukrainian |
| `directions.md` | What each career direction actually requires | Ukrainian |
| `emphasis.md` | Floor-and-surplus plan, reviewed monthly | English |

Subject plans and lesson sequences go here too, as they get written.

## How the three fit together

```text
state/aspirations.md   what he says he wants, re-asked monthly
        |
        v
curriculum/directions.md   what that direction actually takes
        |
        v
curriculum/why/<subject>.md   why today's topic matters, honestly
        |
        v
curriculum/emphasis.md   where this month's extra hours go
```

The chain runs one way. A stated direction changes the *answers* and the
*surplus*; it never changes the floor, and it never removes a subject. See
`emphasis.md` for why that constraint is deliberate.

## The one rule that holds all of it up

Never invent a reason. A topic with no strong application gets the honest
answer — it is in the programme, it is a prerequisite — and that answer costs
nothing. A single fabricated application makes every real one sound like sales
talk, and there is no way back from that with a 12-year-old.
