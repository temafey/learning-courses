# Aspirations

What he says he wants to do, asked once a month. Kept as a history, not
overwritten — the changes are the interesting part.

Nothing here is a decision. At twelve, a stated direction is raw material for
answering «навіщо це вчити», and a way to notice what stays constant while the
labels change.

## Standing interests

Filled by the parent, not by the check-in. What survives across changing
answers — the labels move faster than the interest under them.

| Interest | First seen | Still there | Shows up as |
|---|---|---|---|

«Полетіти на Марс» in October and «винаходити машини» in January are the same
underlying interest wearing two labels. That underlying one is what should
steer the surplus in `curriculum/emphasis.md`, not the month's headline.

## Rules for whoever runs the check-in

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Record his words verbatim | A tidied-up version is the parent's idea, not his |
| Never evaluate the answer, in either direction | Enthusiasm and scepticism both teach him to give the expected answer |
| Never mention last month's answer during the questions | It anchors him to what he said before |
| Ask what he *did*, not only what he wants | Behaviour is a better signal than aspiration, and he answers it more honestly |
